It's Sunday, I'm looking forward to my second binge of Umamusume Cindarella Gray, but a thought came to my head. Not to sound glum, but since moving away from esports and the whimsical life of going out and meeting friends all the time, I've noticed my interests and hobbies have diminished. From what I can tell, this just seems to happen as one gets older. The weird thing that occured to me though, was not having an idea of something to build and it occured to me, not in a way which is worrying but it did all the same, I usually aim to learn things to which I can apply to. Esports and just general interests, my skills have been honed specifically for those things, which are great reasons and I don't regret that at all. What has occured to me, is I've learnt these things while building something which has always been the case.

### Cardsbolt has been great

Before I get to that, I just wanted to say I'm very happy with what I've done with Cardsbolt. While I've made a lot of things, this is something I can smile about. It's made and I can expand it to do more and more things but what it's doing now, I'm very happy with and will work more on it throughout the weeks.

Cardsbolt is interesting for it's a project which well, works. While a lot of people create things which may use other systems or infrastructure of others, I do feel I've built this from the ground up and I'm very happy with that. As said in my previous post, I allocate 10 mins a day on Cardsbolt to make sure the system works correctly. The funny thing is, it actually only take 2 minutes. As I add more products, this will only add more and more and as I mature the application to get all the smaller things sorted, so it becomes cheaper and more effective. I'm very happy with what I've done and will figure out cheaper ways to work on it further, but for now, I feel no rush at all as it's working.

That's the thing though. Now that the project is not needing all the time in the world to be operational, well I have no idea what to do next. This returns us to the previous point. If I've nothing to apply my skills on, then that's really it.

### A small bit of history

Usually I've had something to push to. For a majority of my life till 2023, it was esports. I wanted to get involved with it but ultimately, I realised being a general manager wasn't for me and I'm not too sure why I didn't keep to the idea of being a manager, but I recall very well, the fun had ended and I stopped that. I was doing SEO and while I enjoy it for my own things, I seen programming as something that allowed me to do SEO better, and then I realised that rather than using progrmaming as a way to just do SEO better, I was able to build things. I built many of projects I was interested in which I thought others would be interested in. Skysanner Plus, Factsbolt, Jobsbolt and now Cardsbolt. When I'm focused on something, that's me I'm solid.

I could artifically put more and more of that into Cardsbolt and I generally do want to do more with it but that's not the point I'm attempting to illustrate. What I'm saying is, that need to be hyper focused isn't there now and it's occured to me, I dont have much in the way of life that's pushing me to go, I need to build x or do y or make sure z happens. Usually there's something driving me, that makes me learn skills and apply them. For the first time, sitting here on Sunday afternoon in 2026, I don't have that.

While I enjoy anime, I can't really think of something that I want to commit to. I do want to build something that would be useful, but right now I'm not in the mood to figure that out. Gaming, it just feels like a sink to me. I'm not good enough anymore to play competitive games which and fun games are like hmm to me. Maybe I'm just being tight.

Now I usually know what I need to learn or at least thought I did. Given the circumstances though and where I'm at today, there might be some truth that I might be going about things wrong. What I've been learning and having to do, have been influenced by things I was doing but that's not the case right now. So the thought occured to me.

### Maybe I should look to formal education?

I don't see how this is a bad idea, though being told that programmers are going to be out of jobs in the next few years, it might be a terrible idea but I don't think that's entirely correct. However I do think there's gapping holes in my knowledge which I've attempted for years to sort out on my own. Can I build an application from start to finish, yes. Could I do it better, yes. I've used applied learning as the reason to learn things and I think that was the right direction for me personally for a lot of it but now I'm thinking, to get it over the edge, the last bits, to bring it altogether via a degree. I'm not sure if that's the right way but something tells me, that either I make the decision to learn directly on the pure basis of skills on my own and go for it, or to get a degree as quickly as possible and use that as the proof of knowledge.

I need to think more on this but I don't think I'm wrong. I'm happy with what I've done all of this time. I know I've been able to build systems but I don't think I have a concreate understanding of everything I've done within the space of Software Engineering. What I'm attempting to say is, I don't think there would be much to do at this point. I think it would be very little.
